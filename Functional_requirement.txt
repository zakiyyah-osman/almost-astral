INTERPLANETARY SURVIVAL GUIDE: MARTIAN MAP
Functional and non-functional requirements
Team Almost Astral - NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026
=====================================================================

ASSUMPTIONS
- What it is: a web app on a tablet or habitat screen, used to plan
  and carry out a trip on Mars, on foot and by rover.
- Mission plan:
    - The landing point changes with each mission.
    - The destination, J0, is fixed.
    - The route crosses hazardous ground.
    - The crew can add side quests on the way, for example collecting
      minerals in an area rich in them.
- Where: Jezero crater, on the western delta front and the crater floor
  below it.
- Science goal: study the delta's layered rocks and their carbonate and
  clay minerals, for signs of past water and habitability.
- Crew: two astronauts on foot, supported by a rover.
- Time frame: mostly fixed data, plus a time window of changing data
  (radiation, weather) that can be replayed.
- Priority:
    MUST   = needed for the demo
    SHOULD = important, but not essential
    COULD  = only if time is left
- "Passes when" = the test that proves a requirement is done.


---------------------------------------------------------------------
0. MISSION SETTINGS
---------------------------------------------------------------------
FR-0  MUST  Mission settings
  - Each mission sets:
      - landing point
      - J0
      - crew size
      - time limit per outing
      - dose limit
      - walking speed
      - number of sample containers
  - The settings are saved with the plan.
  - Passes when: changing any setting updates the route and every
    check within 1 second.

FR-0a MUST  What is at J0
  - Each mission states whether J0 has a shelter.
  - If it has none, the plan includes the way back, and the safety
    reserve applies to the whole round trip.


---------------------------------------------------------------------
1. MAP AND NAVIGATION
---------------------------------------------------------------------
FR-1  MUST  Zoom and pan
  - The map zooms from about 50 m across to the whole site.
  - Below 50 m, it shows more detail where the full HiRISE image
    (25 cm per pixel) is loaded.

FR-2  MUST  Orbit image as the base map
  - The base map is a HiRISE image from orbit.
  - COULD: Ingenuity aerial photos and Perseverance rover photos, where
    they cover the site.

FR-3  MUST  Map tools
  - A scale bar and a north arrow.
  - The coordinates of any point the user points at.

FR-4  MUST  Coverage of the whole trip
  - The map covers the landing point, J0 and the land between them.
  - The team's scripts can build a new site from any centre point where
    a HiRISE height model exists.
  - Any point outside the data is shown as "outside coverage", and no
    route is planned there.

FR-5  COULD  High-contrast and low-light mode
  - A helmet-display (HUD) style view and a low-light mode.


---------------------------------------------------------------------
2. DATA LAYERS
---------------------------------------------------------------------
FR-6  MUST  At least 3 NASA missions
  - At least three kinds of data, from different NASA missions.

FR-7  MUST  Terrain
  - Height, slope and shaded relief, from the HiRISE height model.
  - MOLA is used where HiRISE has none, and is labelled as coarse.

FR-8  MUST  Minerals
  - A mineral map from CRISM: carbonate, clay, olivine.
  - Each mineral area shows how sure the detection is.
  - CRISM pixels are about 18 m, so it shows areas, not single rocks.
  - SHOULD: Perseverance chemistry (SuperCam, PIXL, SHERLOC) at rover
    sample points inside the site; if there are none, the app says so.

FR-9  SHOULD  Ground temperature
  - Thermal inertia from THEMIS.
  - Ground temperature from THEMIS (orbit) or from MEDA's
    ground-temperature sensor (surface).

FR-10 MUST  Radiation
  - A surface dose rate, labelled as an ESTIMATE: it comes from
    Curiosity's RAD at Gale crater.
  - Solar storm (solar particle event) warnings from DONKI or MAVEN.

FR-11 SHOULD  Weather
  - Air temperature, pressure, wind and dust from Perseverance's MEDA,
    live or replayed.
  - Where data is missing, the app shows "no data".
  - MEDA's wind sensor was damaged in 2022, so wind has gaps.

FR-12 MUST  Layer controls
  - Turn each layer on or off and set its opacity.

FR-13 COULD  Compare two layers
  - Show two layers side by side, or with a swipe slider.

FR-14 MUST  Layer information
  - Every layer shows:
      - a legend and units
      - the mission it comes from
      - the date the data was taken
      - its resolution


---------------------------------------------------------------------
3. SAFETY AND HAZARDS
---------------------------------------------------------------------
FR-15 MUST  Where you can walk
  - A map of safe, careful and no-go ground, made from slope, rocks and
    ground type.
  - The limits are in one settings file (terrain/thresholds.toml):
      - careful above 10 degrees
      - no-go above 20 degrees
  - These limits are team assumptions, and the app labels them that way.

FR-16 MUST  Hazard types
  - The app marks:
      - steep slopes
      - boulders and outcrops
      - loose sand and dunes
      - cliffs and craters
  - Hazards are found on the fine data (25 cm image, 1 m heights).
  - Each 5 m cell then takes the WORST value found inside it, so a 1 m
    boulder is never lost.

FR-17 MUST  Radiation dose
  - Show the estimated dose for the route, including side quests.
  - Warn when it goes over the mission's dose limit (default 1,000 uSv
    per outing, a team assumption).

FR-18 SHOULD  Weather alerts
  - Show an alert when temperature, dust or wind crosses a set limit,
    or when a solar storm is announced.

FR-19 MUST  Way back
  - Show the distance and walking time back to the rover, the lander,
    or J0 if J0 has a shelter.
  - These update at every point of the route.

FR-20 SHOULD  Suit limits
  - Show suit limits (consumables, time outside) and flag routes that go
    over them.

FR-21 MUST  Turn-back point
  - Mark the point on the route where walking back would use up the
    safety reserve (30% of the time limit by default).
  - It is recomputed every time a side quest is added or removed.
  - From any point on the route, there must be a safe way back that fits
    the reserve.
  - List what makes the crew turn back early: a storm, low consumables,
    an injury, loss of radio.

FR-22 SHOULD  Radio contact
  - Show where along the route the crew has a clear radio line to the
    rover, computed from the height map.
  - Warn about parts of the route that are out of contact, for example
    below the delta cliff.
  - Show the message delay to Earth (3 to 22 minutes).


---------------------------------------------------------------------
4. ROUTE PLANNING
---------------------------------------------------------------------
FR-23 MUST  Route-finder
  - Find a route from the landing point to J0, through the chosen stops.
  - The route never crosses:
      - ground over 20 degrees
      - no-go cells
      - no-data cells
  - Three choices: shortest time, lowest risk, or a balance of both.
  - Radiation is the same across the site, so lowest dose = shortest
    time.
  - Passes when: no route ever crosses a no-go or no-data cell.

FR-24 MUST  Rover legs
  - The rover drives the long parts, using its own slope limits.
  - The crew walks from where the rover drops them off.
  - If the trip does not fit one outing, the app splits it into rover
    drives and walks, or into several sols.

FR-25 MUST  Pick any point
  - The user can pick the landing point, J0 and stops anywhere on the
    map, and the route updates.

FR-26 SHOULD  Drag to edit
  - Drag points on the route; hazards and figures update at once.

FR-27 MUST  Route figures
  - Distance, height profile, slope profile and time, for each leg and
    for the whole trip.
  - Passes when: all of them update within 1 second of changing the
    route.

FR-28 SHOULD  Walking speed and load
  - Walking speed is set per mission (default 2.7 km/h, a NASA Apollo
    planning figure).
  - The speed drops as the crew carries more load, including samples.

FR-29 SHOULD  Landing and parking spots
  - Suggest flat spots for the lander or the rover, with clear flatness
    rules.
  - Check any landing point the user picks against the same rules.

FR-30 COULD  Two-person plans
  - Separate or shared routes for the two astronauts, with a safety
    distance between them.

FR-31 COULD  Best time to go out
  - Suggest a time window for the outing from temperature and daylight.
  - Needs real weather data first.

FR-32 SHOULD  Mars time
  - Show the sol and Mars local time.
  - The user can set the start time of the outing.

FR-33 SHOULD  Save and export
  - Save and load a plan.
  - Export it as PDF and GeoJSON.

FR-34 SHOULD  Printed crew briefing
  - A one-page briefing to print, with:
      - the route
      - the stops and side quests
      - the times
      - the limits
      - the turn-back point


---------------------------------------------------------------------
5. SIDE QUESTS (SAMPLES ON THE WAY TO J0)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
FR-35 MUST  Find nearby targets
  - List the mineral areas (carbonate, clay, olivine) within a set
    number of minutes of the route.
  - Each area shows its mineral, its size and how sure the detection is.

FR-36 MUST  Cost of a detour
  - For each side quest, show:
      - extra minutes
      - extra distance
      - extra dose
      - extra risk
  - Also show whether the whole plan still fits the time limit and the
    reserve.

FR-37 MUST  Must and optional stops
  - J0 is a MUST stop; side quests are OPTIONAL.
  - When time, dose or weather runs short, the app drops optional stops
    first, lowest science value first, and says which ones it dropped.

FR-38 SHOULD  Sample load
  - Each sample adds weight and uses one container.
  - The plan warns when the containers run out or the load slows the
    walk past the time limit.

FR-39 SHOULD  Sample record
  - Each sample stores:
      - its position and time
      - a photo
      - notes
      - the mineral it was meant to be
  - The crew confirms or corrects the mineral on site.


---------------------------------------------------------------------
6. SCIENCE AND WHAT THE CREW WILL SEE
---------------------------------------------------------------------
FR-40 MUST  Stop details
  - Open a stop to see where it is, its geology and its minerals.

FR-41 MUST  Ground-level photos
  - Show Perseverance photos (Mastcam-Z, Navcam) taken near the chosen
    stop.
  - If none exist nearby, the app says so.

FR-42 COULD  3D preview
  - A simulated first-person view of the ground from any point on the
    route.

FR-43 SHOULD  Points of interest
  - Mark places of science interest (delta outcrops, carbonate rock,
    sample sites), each with a short description.

FR-44 COULD  Notes
  - Add notes to stops during or after the outing.


---------------------------------------------------------------------
7. LIVE USE DURING THE TRIP
---------------------------------------------------------------------
FR-45 SHOULD  Crew position
  - Show where the crew is and how far along the route they are.

FR-46 COULD  Re-plan
  - Re-plan the route when the crew leaves it or a hazard changes.

FR-47 SHOULD  Works offline
  - The app works with no connection, from data stored in advance.

FR-48 SHOULD  Demo replay
  - A replay of about 30 seconds that walks the route, does a side
    quest, and sets off a storm warning that triggers the turn-back.


---------------------------------------------------------------------
8. DATA AND SYSTEM
---------------------------------------------------------------------
FR-49 MUST  Public NASA sources
  - Data comes from public NASA sources (PDS, Mars Trek, USGS
    Astrogeology, PDS Geosciences Node), each credited in the app.

FR-50 MUST  One grid
  - All layers line up on the same Mars coordinate system and cell size
    (5 m).

FR-51 MUST  "No data" is never "safe"
  - Missing or coarse data is shown as "no data", never as safe.
  - "No mineral detected" looks different from "no mineral data".

FR-52 MUST  Label every number
  - Each figure is tagged as REAL, CALCULATED, ESTIMATE, ASSUMPTION or
    PLACEHOLDER.


=====================================================================
NON-FUNCTIONAL REQUIREMENTS
=====================================================================
NFR-1  Speed
  - The first map is ready in under 2 seconds on an iPad (2020) over
    Wi-Fi.
  - A new route is computed in under 1 second.

NFR-2  Gloves
  - Touch targets are at least 64 px (about 15 mm).
  - The main actions work with one tap, without precise dragging.

NFR-3  Readable outside
  - Text contrast is at least 7:1, so it reads in bright light.
  - Key numbers are at least 24 px.

NFR-4  Colour blindness
  - Hazard classes differ by pattern as well as colour.
  - Every colour scale is safe for colour-blind users.

NFR-5  Safety first
  - When data is missing or uncertain, the app takes the safer choice:
    "no data" is treated as no-go, and an estimate is never shown as
    measured.

NFR-6  Data quality
  - Every layer shows its date, its resolution and how reliable it is.

NFR-7  Units
  - Metric only.
  - Times are in minutes, and in sols where needed.

NFR-8  Access
  - Works with a keyboard and a screen reader.

NFR-9  Offline
  - The whole app and its data fit in one download, under 50 MB.

NFR-10 Version
  - The app shows the version and date of its data.

NFR-11 Any browser
  - Runs in current Chrome, Safari and Firefox, with no install and no
    login.

NFR-12 Can be checked
  - The same inputs always give the same route.
  - All limits are in settings files, not hidden in code.


=====================================================================
WHAT CHANGED FROM THE PREVIOUS VERSION
=====================================================================
- Requirements renumbered FR-0 to FR-52.
- Mission plan added: the landing point changes, J0 is fixed, and side
  quests are optional.
- New:
    - mission settings (FR-0)
    - what is at J0 (FR-0a)
    - map covers the whole trip (FR-4)
    - side quests (FR-35 to FR-39)
- Rover legs raised to MUST (FR-24), because the trip can be longer
  than one walk.
- Radio contact raised to SHOULD (FR-22), because the route can pass
  below the delta cliff, out of sight of the rover.
- Turn-back point is recomputed with every side quest (FR-21).
- Hazards are found on fine data and kept at their worst value per cell
  (FR-16), so boulders are not lost.
- Mineral areas show how sure the detection is (FR-8, FR-35).
- Non-functional requirements made testable (NFR-1 to NFR-12).
